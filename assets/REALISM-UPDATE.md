# BMX Cruisers: Course One realism update

All dressing is visual. Original instructions, course order, collision masks, obstacle gaps, score and finish logic remain authoritative.

The ten additional realism improvements are covered by:

1. Soft daylight: broad moving cloud shade, a wider sun source, ambient sky capture and contact shadows.
2. Worn trail: scanned asphalt cracks/variation, irregular low gravel and softer broken verges.
3. Contact: tyre tread and spokes rotate together; original-path tyre contact, foot/skate contact poses and subtle rider vibration keep the character grounded.
4. Rider motion: skeletal arm/leg IK, independent riding/standing/wheelchair poses, lean, hand/finger adjustments and weight shifts.
5. Weathered obstacles: irregular timber, scanned stone/wood, metal fittings and detailed wheel/drivetrain/brake parts.
6. Movement debris: a short visual grit/dust wake behind the rider.
7. Sound placement: attenuated waterside sound sources supplement wind/birds, rolling sound and collision effects.
8. Small details: fallen leaves and twigs, gravel, seed heads/cattails and shallow-water stones/driftwood.
9. Background: irregular distant tree lines, mild atmospheric haze and landscape depth.
10. Finishing: restrained color grading, darker patchy grass, varied roughness, natural highlight response and readable course obstacles.

Additional requested features: three expanding rings per water site, stronger foliage wind, 18 birds/ducks, meadow relief that fades away from the trail and water, boundary rope/stones outside the original sprite limits, boy/girl riders, casual/extreme outfits, eight vehicle/helmet colors, six cosmetic vehicle styles, persistent selections, keyboard menus, cover-inspired splash/logo, instructions, Credits, clean/raspy/overdriven guitar phrases and colored finish fireworks with separate burst sounds.

The wildlife, six vehicles and protective gear are custom lightweight models. This is a working real-time game; the earlier AI concept render remains an art reference rather than a claim of pixel-identical rendering quality.
